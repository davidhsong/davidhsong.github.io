Field notes

Conference notes on infrastructure, open-source machine learning, and the distance between a demo and a durable system.

September 29 - October 1, 2026 · San Francisco

CoreWeave Fully Connected 2026

01

1 / 4

Fully Connected made one idea feel concrete: once AI leaves a notebook, infrastructure becomes part of the product. Model quality matters, but so do the systems that keep training, serving, and evaluation fast, observable, secure, and affordable.

What I took away

  • End-to-end performance beats isolated benchmarks. Compute, networking, storage, orchestration, and inference strategy all shape latency, throughput, and cost.
  • Inference is a design choice. Serverless, dedicated, and self-hosted serving trade convenience for control in different ways; the right answer depends on workload shape and service guarantees.
  • Agents need an evaluation loop. Tracing, production-derived test sets, regression checks, and quality gates are what turn an impressive workflow into a system people can trust.

The biggest shift in my thinking was to treat observability and evaluation as product features, not cleanup work. That is the lens I want to bring into future AI projects.

October 20 - 21, 2026 · San Jose

PyTorch Conference North America 2026

02

1 / 1

PyTorch Conference landed at the right moment for me. I had just started using PyTorch to classify human activity from Wi-Fi CSI data, so the talks connected framework internals and frontier-scale systems back to the training loop I was building myself.

What I took away

  • Portability now spans the whole stack. PyTorch is being pushed across GPUs, TPUs, Trainium, custom accelerators, and on-device runtimes without giving up the developer experience that made it useful.
  • Performance work is measurement work. Profilers, compiler tooling, distributed communication, CI, and observability matter because they make bottlenecks visible and improvements repeatable.
  • Open source is an operating model. Release engineering, compatibility testing, maintainers, and community feedback are as important as any single feature.

I left wanting to be more disciplined about experiments: save checkpoints, record the data conditions behind every metric, and make inference on a new sample as reproducible as training.
